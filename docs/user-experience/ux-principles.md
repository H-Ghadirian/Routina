# UX Principles

These principles guide product choices across every Routina surface. They describe the experience a person should receive, not how the app is implemented.

## P-01 — Start with the person's outcome

Organize flows around what the person is trying to accomplish. A screen, feature, or field is useful only when it makes a real situation easier.

Ask: **What can the person understand or accomplish after this change that they could not do as well before?**

## P-02 — Make the simple path immediate and the deep path available

Common actions such as capturing, finding, completing, and planning should be quick. Additional detail should appear when requested or when it becomes relevant, without hiding an essential consequence.

## P-03 — Keep concepts distinct

Do not merge concepts merely because they both involve a date or status. Availability, intention, schedule, deadline, reminder, completion, focus, and history should remain understandable on their own.

## P-04 — Represent reality honestly

Routina should distinguish what was planned, inferred, assumed, completed, missed, canceled, and corrected. A polished summary is never more important than a truthful one.

## P-05 — Reduce pressure, not agency

Use hierarchy, defaults, suggestions, and filtering to reduce cognitive load. Do not make a choice on the person's behalf when the evidence is uncertain or the tradeoff is personal.

## P-06 — Prefer recognition over instruction

Controls should look and behave like their purpose. Use familiar platform patterns, clear labels, previews, and contextual disclosure before adding paragraphs of instructional copy.

## P-07 — Preserve context and offer recovery

Navigation should keep the person oriented. Drafts, selection, search context, and history should survive ordinary interruptions when practical. Destructive or irreversible actions need deliberate confirmation and a safe alternative when one exists.

## P-08 — Make the whole visible control usable

Anything that looks clickable should respond across its full visible surface. Controls need comfortable targets, keyboard support where expected, readable contrast, meaningful labels, and layouts that adapt to text size and available space.

## P-09 — Stay responsive at real-life scale

Typing, scrolling, selecting, and opening details should feel immediate with years of history, not only in a new account. The person should not have to understand background refresh or synchronization work to use the interface smoothly.

## P-10 — Earn trust around personal data

Explain permissions in context, ask only for capabilities the available feature needs, report synchronization truthfully, keep diagnostics free of personal content, and put backup before destructive data operations.

## P-11 — Be coherent across platforms, not mechanically identical

The meaning of tasks, plans, outcomes, and history should stay consistent. Semantic cues such as completion, warning, cancellation, and reversal should retain their meaning across platforms, while navigation and interaction use the native strengths of iPhone, iPad, and Mac.

## P-12 — Keep unavailable and experimental experiences honest

Do not advertise a path that cannot work in the current build. Experimental features should be clearly gated, and production should present a complete experience without relying on hidden development controls.
