# Users and Needs

## Evidence Status

This is a working product model derived from Routina's existing behavior, product decisions, and use-case discussions. It is not yet a claim of formal user-research validation.

When interviews, support conversations, usability studies, or repeated real-world feedback confirm or challenge an assumption, record the evidence and revise this page. Avoid demographic personas unless evidence shows that a demographic distinction changes the experience.

## Who Routina Is For

Routina is for a person who wants one trustworthy place to manage intended work and understand what actually happened.

That person may have one-off tasks, repeating responsibilities, flexible routines, fixed commitments, and periods when capacity changes. They may capture quickly on iPhone, plan or review more deeply on Mac, and return after interruptions or days away. They should not need to maintain a complicated productivity system before Routina becomes useful.

## Core Contexts

- **Capture:** Something matters and must be saved before it is forgotten.
- **Orient:** The person opens Routina and needs to understand what deserves attention now.
- **Plan:** The person wants to shape a realistic day without confusing intention with obligation.
- **Act:** The person wants to focus on chosen work and reduce distractions.
- **Record reality:** Work, rest, and meaningful context should reflect what actually happened, including corrections made later.
- **Review:** The person wants to understand a day or period without reconstructing it from memory.
- **Adapt:** The person wants the system to change with their routines, workload, and capacity without losing history.
- **Recover:** The person returns after interruption, missed work, a device change, or a mistake and needs a safe path forward.

## Core Needs

### N-01 — Capture without friction

**Need:** I need to save an intention quickly, while still being able to add meaningful detail when it matters.

**Successful outcome:** A simple item takes seconds to capture. Dates, recurrence, estimates, links, tags, notes, and other details remain available progressively rather than blocking the first save.

### N-02 — Keep different kinds of time honest

**Need:** I need to distinguish when something is available, when I intend to do it, when it is due, when I want a reminder, and when it actually happened.

**Successful outcome:** Changing one kind of time does not silently rewrite another. Routina uses clear language and previews the consequence of scheduling choices.

### N-03 — See what matters now

**Need:** I need a manageable view of actionable work, not an undifferentiated catalog of everything I might ever do.

**Successful outcome:** Current work is easy to scan, unavailable or intentionally hidden work stays out of the way, and search or filters can recover it when needed.

### N-04 — Choose with limited capacity

**Need:** When several tasks are possible, I need help choosing based on my available time, energy, urgency, importance, pressure, and thinking capacity.

**Successful outcome:** Routina reduces comparison effort, explains the relevant signals, and leaves the final choice with the person.

### N-05 — Make a realistic plan

**Need:** I need to shape a day around available time and fixed commitments without turning every intention into a rigid deadline.

**Successful outcome:** The plan combines time-specific commitments with flexible work, supports adjustment, and preserves the original meaning of each task.

### N-06 — Focus on the chosen work

**Need:** Once I choose, I need a clear working session with optional protection from distracting apps or websites.

**Successful outcome:** Starting, pausing, resuming, finishing, or correcting a session is understandable, and the resulting history remains connected to the work when appropriate.

### N-07 — Keep a truthful history

**Need:** I need Routina to distinguish completed, missed, canceled, assumed, and corrected activity instead of forcing reality into a success-only record.

**Successful outcome:** The timeline is credible. Corrections do not erase context, synthetic activity is distinguishable from confirmed activity, and later review does not overstate what happened.

### N-08 — Learn without being judged

**Need:** I need useful patterns about my work, focus, routines, and wellbeing without pressure to maintain perfect streaks or expose irrelevant empty reports.

**Successful outcome:** Reviews show evidence-backed patterns for the selected period, explain their scope, and remain calm when data is sparse.

### N-09 — Adapt without starting over

**Need:** My responsibilities and capacity change. I need to pause, archive, reschedule, reorganize, or refine items without losing their history.

**Successful outcome:** Changes are reversible where practical, destructive actions are explicit, and old records retain their meaning.

### N-10 — Control complexity

**Need:** I need a useful default experience and the ability to reveal advanced organization only when it solves a real problem.

**Successful outcome:** Common actions stay obvious. Advanced filters, metadata, experiments, and customization do not make everyday use feel like system administration.

### N-11 — Trust continuity and privacy

**Need:** I need confidence that my personal history is private, recoverable, synchronized honestly, and not silently discarded.

**Successful outcome:** Routina is explicit about permissions, backup, synchronization, destructive actions, and diagnostics. Sensitive content is not included in support data without clear intent.

### N-12 — Move between devices without relearning the product

**Need:** I need capture, planning, and review to feel coherent across iPhone, iPad, and Mac while respecting what each platform is good at.

**Successful outcome:** Concepts and outcomes stay consistent, navigation follows platform conventions, and device-specific differences feel purposeful rather than arbitrary.

### N-13 — Understand unfamiliar features without guessing

**Need:** When I encounter an unfamiliar Routina label, number, or workspace, I need an explanation grounded in what the app actually means.

**Successful outcome:** I can ask a connected AI a natural-language product question, receive a concise answer that names platform and availability limits, and understand what the feature changes or leaves untouched without exposing my task data merely to obtain help.

## Product Boundary

Routina should help a person remember, choose, plan, act, and learn. It should not make automatic judgments about the person's worth, present estimates as facts, or disguise inferred activity as confirmed history.
