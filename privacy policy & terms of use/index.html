Your data

Privacy Policy

Summary: Routina has no advertising SDK, analytics SDK, or developer-operated generative-AI service. Routina does not operate a developer-owned account or content server. Your Routina content is stored on your device and, when available, in your private iCloud account. When configured in a distributed build, limited crash diagnostics are sent to Google Firebase Crashlytics so the developer can diagnose failures and improve reliability.

Information you create

Routina can store information you choose to enter, including todos, routines, schedules, tags, priorities, checklists, comments, notes, goals, events, focus history, completion history, saved places, sleep or away records, links, images, files, and voice notes. Some categories are experimental or unavailable in the current production apps but can remain in synchronized data and backups for compatibility. This content is used only to provide the features you request inside the app.

Local storage and iCloud

Routina stores data locally on your device. When iCloud synchronization is available, Routina uses Apple CloudKit to synchronize data through the Apple ID signed in on your device. CloudKit processing is governed by Apple's privacy practices. Routina's developer does not operate a separate server that receives a copy of your task database.

Crash reporting

When Firebase Crashlytics is configured in the distributed iOS or macOS app, crash reporting is automatic. A report can include the crash stack, relevant application state, app version, device model, operating-system information, and installation identifiers used by Firebase to process and group reports. Routina adds only the platform, the app variant, and a bounded trail of general interaction categories such as navigation, scrolling, filtering, or task actions.

Routina does not set a Crashlytics user ID and does not intentionally add task titles, descriptions, search text, record identifiers, account details, locations, attachments, or other user content to crash reports. Google Analytics is not included, and crash diagnostics are not used for advertising or tracking. Google processes and retains this diagnostic information under its Firebase privacy and security practices.

Permissions and device capabilities

Available permissions vary by platform and app version. Current production apps request access only when you use a related released feature:

  • Notifications: used to deliver reminders you enable for tasks, routines, or events.
  • Calendars: used when you choose to review Apple Calendar events before importing selected events as Routina tasks.
  • Photos and files: used only for items you explicitly select and attach to Routina content.
  • App Lock: authentication is performed by the operating system. Routina does not receive or store biometric data.

Development builds can contain experimental microphone access for voice notes, location access for Places, and macOS automation for browser-level blocking. Those features and their permission declarations are omitted from the current production iOS and macOS apps. If one is promoted to a production feature later, this policy will be updated before distribution.

You can review or revoke these permissions at any time in System Settings. Disabling a permission prevents the related feature from working but does not prevent use of unrelated Routina features.

Optional external integrations

If you explicitly enable an integration, Routina may communicate directly from your device with the selected service:

  • GitHub or GitLab: Routina can retrieve contribution statistics. A personal access token you provide is stored in the device Keychain and can be removed from Routina's settings.
  • Microsoft Outlook: when this integration is offered and you choose to connect it, Microsoft authentication and Microsoft Graph are used to review calendar events before importing selected events as tasks.
  • Mac AI connections: Routina's local, read-only helper can answer product-help questions without personal task access. If you separately enable read-only task access, Routina writes a private local snapshot that can include task names, schedules, dates, tags, descriptions, notes, links, goals, places, and progress. The helper can return relevant details to your chosen AI client, and that client may send them to its own AI provider. Routina does not proxy those requests through a developer-operated AI service. Turning task access off deletes the local snapshot; removing the connection from the AI client is a separate action.
  • Public link titles: on macOS, pasting a public web URL into Quick Add can contact that website directly from your device to retrieve page metadata for an editable task title. Routina avoids this automatic request for obviously local or credential-bearing URLs.

Use of an external service is also subject to that provider's terms and privacy policy. Routina does not sell information received through an integration.

Sharing

If you use invitation-only iCloud sharing, the people you invite can access the content you share with them. Sharing is handled through Apple CloudKit. Routina does not provide a public content feed or advertising profile.

Retention and deletion

  • You can delete individual Routina items from within the app.
  • You can remove saved GitHub or GitLab connections in Settings, which removes their stored access tokens from Keychain.
  • Settings > iCloud & Backup provides controls to export a backup and delete Routina data from the device and iCloud.
  • Crash diagnostics are retained and deleted under Google Firebase's published retention practices. They are not part of Routina's local or iCloud data-deletion control.
  • Removing the app deletes its local app container, but synchronized iCloud data may remain until you delete it using Routina's data-deletion control.

Because the developer does not maintain a separate Routina account or database, the developer generally cannot identify or delete an individual user's private iCloud records on the user's behalf.

Children's privacy

Routina is a general productivity app and is not directed to children under 13. Routina does not knowingly collect children's personal information on a developer-operated service.
